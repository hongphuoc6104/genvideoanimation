# Motion Grammar

## Principle
Motion should communicate cause, intent, weight, attention, and hierarchy.

## Primary action arc
A strong action often follows:
1. preparation / anticipation
2. launch
3. acceleration
4. main action
5. deceleration
6. contact / peak
7. overshoot
8. settle
9. secondary response

Not every action needs all nine stages.

## Overlapping action
Different body parts should rarely begin and end on the same frame.
Typical lead/lag order:
- eyes/gaze lead
- head follows
- torso follows
- arms/wings respond
- tail/hair/accessories trail

Use small frame offsets.

## Spacing
Avoid uniform movement over time.
Use tighter spacing near holds/settles and larger spacing during fast travel.

## Holds
Readable animation needs holds. Do not animate continuously just to keep the screen busy.

## Character acting
A character entering the frame should not merely translate.
Examples:
- fly: wing cycle + arced trajectory + body pitch + head stabilization + landing sequence
- walk: contact/down/passing/up poses + counter-swing + torso bob + head stabilization
- reaction: gaze → head → torso → expression → secondary body response

## Secondary motion
Use breathing, blink, micro gaze, cloth/hair/tail follow-through, subtle body sway.
Secondary motion must be subordinate to primary action.

## Camera
Camera is another actor. It can anticipate, follow, reveal, or settle.
Do not add random drift as a substitute for intentional camera work.

## Graphic motion
Infographics need the same principles:
- anticipation before reveal
- directional continuity
- clear hierarchy
- sequence rather than simultaneous appearance
- object persistence across transitions when possible
